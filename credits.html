Data Credits

Every map made in Maposaic is drawn from open data. This page lists each source, what it is used for, and how to cite it. Maps you export carry a one-line credit that points here.

Citing a map made in Maposaic

The credit line printed on every exported map reads:

Made in Maposaic · Data: Natural Earth, Pleiades · diboewdip.github.io/mapforge/credits

For a bibliography, a student or teacher can cite a Maposaic map as:

Map title. Map created with Maposaic, <https://diboewdip.github.io/mapforge/>. Base map data: Natural Earth (public domain). Ancient place data: Pleiades, CC BY 3.0. [Date made.]

Map data

Natural Earth

Public domain — no permission needed

Used for nearly everything you see on a live map: coastlines and land, oceans, lakes, rivers and lake centerlines, country boundaries, United States state boundaries, ocean and sea name positions, the shaded relief, and the modern cities in the Find a City tool (Natural Earth's "populated places"). Maposaic uses the 1:110m, 1:50m, and 1:10m scale datasets, switching between them as you zoom.

Natural Earth. Free vector and raster map data. https://www.naturalearthdata.com/. Public domain.

Natural Earth's own suggested credit: "Made with Natural Earth. Free vector and raster map data @ naturalearthdata.com." Terms: naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use.

Pleiades: A Gazetteer of Past Places

Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 (CC BY 3.0)

Used for the ancient places in the Find a City tool — the locations and names of cities of the ancient world. Where Pleiades lists several variant names for one place (for example "Uruk/Orchoe/Erech"), Maposaic shows the first. Pleiades is a joint project of the Institute for the Study of the Ancient World at New York University and the Ancient World Mapping Center at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill.

Roger Bagnall, et al. (eds.), Pleiades: A Gazetteer of Past Places, 2016, https://pleiades.stoa.org/ [Accessed: August 2026].

License: Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 United States. Individual place entries carry their own suggested citations on pleiades.stoa.org/places.
